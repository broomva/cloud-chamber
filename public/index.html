What to look for

  • Alpha particles leave short, bright tracks: about 3.5 to 6 cm, dead straight. They ionize so densely that the droplets compete for vapor and stay small, and the whole column sinks together in a second or two.
  • Muons from cosmic rays leave long, thin, straight tracks, usually slanting down from above.
  • Electrons leave thin tracks that wander and curl as they slow, because single collisions with nuclei turn them.
  • The plate has to be cold. With the top at 20 °C, warm the plate past about -21 °C and the tracks fade out within a degree or two: the vapor is no longer supersaturated enough for ions to start droplets. A warmer top supplies more vapor and moves that limit up.
